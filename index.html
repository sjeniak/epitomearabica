01 — Origin

An island known for tea.
A rarer cup from its hills.

Sri Lanka’s reputation was written in tea. Before that, the island was Ceylon coffee — once among the world’s great origins, then nearly lost to leaf rust and the pivot to Camellia. Higher still, in the central highlands, a quieter crop has been tended for generations: Arabica, grown between 1,200 and 1,800 metres, where nights are cool and the morning sits in mist.

What remains is scarce on the global map: specialty lots from shade-grown Kandyan gardens, coffee beside pepper, clove, and nutmeg, not from monoculture plains. The Ceylon name already travels. The bean does not need a blend to introduce itself.

Epitome is that crop, kept as a single expression. No blend to soften the soil. No second roast to hide a fault. Chocolate depth, vanilla warmth, and a clean stone-fruit finish — the character of highland Sri Lanka, packed in one black-and-gold bag.

Ceylon Arabica sits in a balanced middle against the giants: less polarizing than Ethiopia’s floral, high-acid cups, with more sweetness and spice than a standard washed Latin lot. Moderate acidity, a silky body — honest as pour-over, structured enough for espresso. We work with farming families on those slopes and roast in small lots after you reserve. What reaches you is not warehouse coffee. It is the current harvest, roasted for the cup you will actually drink.

Fire, time, and the mountain.

Roasting is close to alchemy: fire, time, and a bean that already knows the mountain. We do not hurry it. We listen for the moment chocolate turns to fruit.

04 — The cup

Four gestures.
One honest pour-over.

You do not need theatre. You need a scale, water just off the boil, and four quiet minutes. This is how we drink it at the house.

I

Eighteen grams

Freshly ground Epitome. Medium-coarse for pour-over, finer for espresso. Grind as you brew.

II

Water at 93°C

Just off the boil. Hotter scorches the chocolate; cooler leaves the fruit closed.

III

The bloom

Pour 50 ml and wait 35 seconds. The bed rises. That is gas leaving, and flavour arriving.

IV

A slow spiral

The remaining 250 ml in calm circles. Three to four minutes in the cup. Drink while it is alive.

From the house

What is Ceylon specialty coffee?

Ceylon specialty coffee is high-grown Arabica from Sri Lanka — a rare origin after the island’s historic shift to tea. Epitome is a single-origin expression of that crop: highland beans, one medium roast, packed as a 500 g bag of whole beans.

How is Sri Lankan Arabica different from Ethiopian coffee?

Ceylon Arabica typically sits in a balanced middle: moderate acidity and a silky, medium-to-full body, rather than Ethiopia’s very bright, floral, tea-like cups. Epitome drinks with chocolate depth, vanilla warmth, and a clean stone-fruit finish, and holds for both pour-over and espresso.

Where is Epitome grown?

In Sri Lanka’s central highlands, between 1,200 and 1,800 metres. The island’s Arabica is largely shade-grown in biodiverse Kandyan gardens — coffee beside pepper, clove, nutmeg, and shade trees — not vast monoculture.
