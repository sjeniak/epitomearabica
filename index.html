01 — Origin

An island known for tea.
A rarer cup from its hills.

Sri Lanka’s reputation was written in tea. Higher still, in the central highlands, a quieter crop has been tended for generations: Arabica, grown between 1,200 and 1,800 metres, where nights are cool and the morning sits in mist.

Epitome is that crop, kept as a single expression. No blend to soften the soil. No second roast to hide a fault. Chocolate depth, vanilla warmth, and a clean stone-fruit finish — the character of highland Sri Lanka, packed in one black-and-gold bag.

We work with farming families on those slopes and roast in small lots after you reserve. What reaches you is not warehouse coffee. It is the current harvest, roasted for the cup you will actually drink.

03 — In the world

A bag built for the table,
not the warehouse.

Morning on the coast. A city bar after dark. The first sip at a wooden table. Epitome is the same coffee in each — highland Arabica, poured without hurry.

The craft

Roasting is close to alchemy: fire, time, and a bean that already knows the mountain. We do not hurry it. We listen for the moment chocolate turns to fruit.

Every Epitome lot is tasted before it is packed. If the cup is not clean, it does not leave the house. That is the whole method.

04 — The cup

Four gestures.
One honest pour-over.

You do not need theatre. You need a scale, water just off the boil, and four quiet minutes. This is how we drink it at the house.

I

Eighteen grams

Freshly ground Epitome. Medium-coarse for pour-over, finer for espresso. Grind as you brew.

II

Water at 93°C

Just off the boil. Hotter scorches the chocolate; cooler leaves the fruit closed.

III

The bloom

Pour 50 ml and wait 35 seconds. The bed rises. That is gas leaving, and flavour arriving.

IV

A slow spiral

The remaining 250 ml in calm circles. Three to four minutes in the cup. Drink while it is alive.
