Four gestures. One honest pour-over.

Published 20 October 2026 Reading 3 min

Pour-over is not theatre. You do not need a machine with a screen. You need a scale, water just off the boil, and four quiet minutes. This is how the house drinks Epitome.

What you need

A kettle, a dripper and its paper filter, a burr grinder, a scale — and eighteen grams of whole-bean Epitome. Grind as you brew, not before: coffee begins to fade the moment it is ground.

The four gestures

I — Eighteen grams

Weigh 18 g of Epitome and grind it medium-coarse — coarser than espresso, finer than French press. The grind decides how fast the water passes, and therefore what it carries away.

II — Water at 93 °C

Bring the water just off the boil, around 93 °C. Hotter scorches the chocolate; cooler leaves the fruit closed.

III — The bloom

Pour 50 ml in a slow, even circle and wait 35 seconds. The bed rises and releases its gas — the bloom. That is flavour arriving.

IV — A slow spiral

Pour the remaining 250 ml in calm spirals, keeping the stream steady. Three to four minutes in the cup. Drink it while it is alive.

Why pour-over suits Epitome

Pour-over gives a clean, transparent cup — and Epitome has nothing to hide. Expect dark chocolate, vanilla, and a clean stone-fruit finish, with moderate acidity and a silky body. The same bag holds for espresso, French press, moka, and cold brew.

You do not need theatre. You need four quiet minutes.

That is the whole method.
